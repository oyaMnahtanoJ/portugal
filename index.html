What to book in advance

Ordered so you part with money as late as safely possible, in case the trip has to be called off. The rule: lock the scarce things now only where cancelling is free, and leave the non-refundable tickets until you're sure you're going. Most are cheap and rarely sell out a month out, so there's genuinely no rush.

Now · free-cancellation only
  • Stay
    Hotels: all of them, on free-cancellation rates. September is peak and the Douro's in harvest, so availability is the real risk; a free-cancel rate locks it at zero cost if plans change.
  • Car
    Hire car, one-way LIS → OPO, estate/SUV, automatic if needed, with the Via Verde transponder. Book a pay-at-desk / free-cancellation rate now to hold the scarce one-way + automatic stock; cancel free if the trip falls through.

The car & the tolls

You have the car for seven days, LIS airport to OPO airport. Two things trip up foreign drivers here: the electronic-only tolls, and turning up with the wrong-size boot.

The vehicle

Collect at LIS airport, Day 6 (Sun) morning; return at OPO airport, Day 12 (Sat) afternoon, straight after the morning hike. Both airport desks keep long daily hours, and you never drive into either city centre. Confirm the one-way drop fee up front.

Your car: a Volvo V60 estate is booked (~519 L under the cover). Three checked cases plus three cabin bags fit, though not as three big hard-shells: confirm it's a guaranteed V60 rather than "or similar", keep at least one cabin bag soft, and on hiking days keep it all below the parcel shelf and out of sight.

Tolls

Portugal has electronic-only motorways with no booths: cameras read a transponder or your number plate, and you can't pay cash. Your route touches them (the A25 near Caramulo and the roads around the Douro and the Barroso).

The fix: take the hire company's Via Verde transponder. It auto-charges every toll to your card, plus a small daily fee. Get it and stop thinking about tolls.

Budget: tolls ~€25–40 (several former toll-only northern motorways went free in 2025, so reconfirm for your route); fuel ~€1.75/L, roughly €120–170 over ~800 km.

Documents

The main driver's credit card (not debit) for the deposit hold, passports, and a driving licence plus the International Driving Permit from Israel. At 30 there's no young-driver surcharge.

Parking & driving

Use hotel car parks and paid town car parks, and pay at the blue-zone meters. Leave nothing on show in the car: your bags sit in it at several points (before check-in on Days 6 and 7, during the Day 8 and Day 12 hikes, and around Coimbra and Caramulo on Day 9), so use guarded car parks and keep everything in the boot. Book the quintas and the Montalegre stay with parking.

Mountain roads (Lousã, Caramulo, the Douro) are winding, so daytime only, and keep the tank above a quarter. And no driving after the Douro tastings: spit at the estate or nominate the day's driver. Emergency number is 112.

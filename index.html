The car & the tolls

You have the car for seven days, LIS airport to OPO airport. Two things trip up foreign drivers here: the electronic-only tolls, and turning up with the wrong-size boot.

The vehicle

Collect at LIS airport, Day 6 (Sun) morning; return at OPO airport, Day 12 (Sat) afternoon, straight after the morning hike. Both airport desks keep long daily hours, and you never drive into either city centre. Confirm the one-way drop fee up front.

Your car: a Volvo V60 estate is booked, ~519 L below the luggage cover. Three checked cases plus three cabin bags may fit out of sight, but only if they aren't all large rigid shells and at least one cabin bag is soft: test-pack the real dimensions before you fly, and confirm the booking guarantees a V60 rather than "or similar". On hiking days everything must sit below the parcel shelf.

Tolls

Portugal has electronic-only motorways with no booths: cameras read a transponder or your number plate, and you can't pay cash. Your route still uses tolled sections, mostly on the western run north and the approaches to Porto; several former toll-only inland motorways, the A25 among them, went free in 2025.

The fix: take the hire company's Via Verde transponder. It auto-charges every toll to your card, plus a small daily fee. Get it and stop thinking about tolls.

Budget: tolls ~€25–40, worth recalculating for the final route nearer the time; fuel ~€1.75/L, roughly €120–160 over ~950 km depending on the engine you're given and how much mountain driving it does, with €200 as the conservative ceiling.

Documents

The main driver's credit card (not debit) for the deposit hold, passports, and a driving licence plus the International Driving Permit from Israel. At 30 there's no young-driver surcharge.

Parking & driving

Use hotel car parks and paid town car parks, and pay at the blue-zone meters. Leave nothing on show in the car: your bags sit in it at several points (before check-in on Days 6 and 7, during the Day 8 and Day 12 hikes, and around Coimbra and Caramulo on Day 9), so use guarded car parks and keep everything in the boot. Book the quintas and the Montalegre stay with parking.

Mountain roads (Lousã, Caramulo, the Douro and the Barroso) are winding, so daytime only, and keep the tank above a quarter; fill up before the Xertelo run on Day 12, as pumps are sparse up there. And no driving after the tastings: spit at the estate or nominate the day's driver, who also skips the Óbidos ginjinha. Emergency number is 112.
